Customer: Acme Technologies
Meeting: Developer Productivity Discussion
Date: 2026-07-14

Participants:
- David Lee, Director of Engineering
- Neha Rao, Staff Software Engineer
- Michael Wong, Engineering Manager
- Priya Shah, Customer Success Manager

Priya:
I'd like to understand where the team is still losing time in the development
workflow.

David:
One thing that comes up repeatedly is legacy code. We have services that were
written years ago and not everyone understands how they work anymore.

Neha:
That's definitely a problem. When I get assigned an issue in one of the older
Java services, I can spend an hour or more just figuring out what the code is
doing before I can make a change.

Michael:
And it's not limited to new developers. Even experienced engineers sometimes
have to trace through several services before they understand the dependency
chain.

Priya:
What do you typically do today when someone needs to understand one of these
services?

Neha:
Mostly search through the repository, look at old documentation, check Jira
tickets, and ask someone who has worked on it before.

David:
The documentation isn't always reliable either. Some of it hasn't been updated
in years.

Michael:
We also have information spread across GitHub, Confluence, Jira, and internal
documents. Finding the right information is probably half the problem.

Priya:
How frequently does this happen?

Neha:
Almost every time someone works on one of the older services. Maybe several
times a week across the team.

David:
I'd be interested in using AI here, but I don't want it confidently explaining
something incorrectly. If we use it, the answers need to be grounded in our
actual code and documentation.

Neha:
Exactly. If it could point me to the relevant code, documentation, and Jira
history while explaining how a service works, that would save me a lot of time.

Michael:
I'd also want to know where the answer came from. Engineers won't trust a
black-box answer.

Priya:
Would you be willing to pilot something with a small engineering group?

David:
Yes. If we can connect it to our internal documentation and repositories and
show that engineers are actually spending less time understanding existing
code, I'd support a pilot.

Neha:
I'd be happy to test it. The source references would be important for me.
