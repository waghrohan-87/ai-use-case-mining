Customer: Acme Technologies
Meeting: Production Engineering Discussion
Date: 2026-07-22

Participants:
- Lisa Morgan, Director of Engineering
- Arjun Nair, Engineering Manager, Platform
- Kevin Zhang, Senior Site Reliability Engineer
- Priya Shah, Customer Success Manager

Priya:
I'd like to understand some of the areas where the engineering team spends
significant time outside of normal feature development.

Lisa:
Production incidents are probably one of the biggest ones. When something
breaks, engineers spend a lot of time trying to understand what happened
before they can actually fix it.

Kevin:
A lot of the investigation is manual. We look through logs, dashboards,
recent deployments, Jira tickets, and previous incident reports.

Arjun:
The difficult part is that the information is spread across several systems.
An engineer might spend 30 or 40 minutes just gathering context before they
start troubleshooting.

Priya:
Does this happen frequently?

Kevin:
We have several incidents every week across our services. Most aren't major,
but even smaller incidents consume engineering time.

Lisa:
The bigger problem is when the person investigating hasn't seen the service
before. They need to understand the architecture and then figure out what
changed.

Kevin:
We have runbooks, but they're not always followed because engineers don't
always know which runbook applies to the incident.

Priya:
Have you considered using AI to help with incident investigation?

Kevin:
Yes, but we'd have to be careful. I wouldn't want an AI system automatically
changing production systems.

Lisa:
Agreed. I'm interested in something that can gather information and help
engineers investigate, but humans need to remain responsible for the final
decision.

Arjun:
If it could look at the alert, recent deployments, relevant logs, similar
past incidents, and the applicable runbook, that would save a lot of time.

Kevin:
It would also be useful if it could identify similar incidents we've had
before. We have years of incident history but engineers don't always know
where to look.

Priya:
What would you measure in a pilot?

Kevin:
Time from alert to initial diagnosis would be a good metric.

Arjun:
Also how often the AI recommendations are useful and how often engineers
have to discard them.

Lisa:
If we can reduce investigation time without increasing operational risk,
I'd support expanding it to more teams.

Priya:
So a pilot could focus on investigation and information gathering, with any
production changes remaining under human approval.

Lisa:
Yes. That would be the right approach.
