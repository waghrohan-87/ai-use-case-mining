Customer: Acme Technologies
Meeting: Engineering Release Process Review
Date: 2026-07-29

Participants:
- Tom Anderson, Director of Engineering
- Kavya Menon, Engineering Manager
- Chris Evans, Senior Developer
- Priya Shah, Customer Success Manager

Priya:
Can you walk me through some of the manual work involved in your release
process today?

Tom:
Release preparation takes more time than it should. The engineering teams
have to gather information from Jira and GitHub and then prepare release
notes for stakeholders.

Kavya:
Every sprint we have developers and engineering managers going through
tickets and pull requests to figure out what actually changed.

Chris:
A lot of the information is already there. We're just manually turning it
into something that product and business teams can understand.

Priya:
How much time does that typically take?

Kavya:
For a larger release, probably several hours across the team. Smaller
releases aren't as bad, but we're doing them frequently.

Tom:
The other issue is consistency. Different teams write release notes in
different formats.

Chris:
Sometimes I end up copying information from five or six Jira tickets into
a document and then rewriting it so it makes sense.

Priya:
Have you tried using AI for this?

Chris:
I've experimented with it personally. It can produce a decent first draft,
but you have to give it the right context.

Kavya:
That's the challenge. If the AI only sees a ticket title, it won't know
what actually changed.

Tom:
If we could connect it to Jira and GitHub and have it generate a draft based
on the actual changes, that would be useful.

Priya:
Would you want it to publish the release notes automatically?

Tom:
No. Someone from engineering should review them first.

Kavya:
I'd also want the output to follow our existing format.

Chris:
And it would be useful if it could identify changes that are important for
customers versus internal technical changes.

Priya:
What would success look like?

Kavya:
Reduce the time spent preparing release notes and improve consistency
between teams.

Tom:
If we can get the first draft down from a few hours to maybe 20 minutes of
review, I'd consider that a success.

Priya:
Would this be suitable for a pilot?

Tom:
Yes. Start with one product team and compare preparation time against our
current process.
