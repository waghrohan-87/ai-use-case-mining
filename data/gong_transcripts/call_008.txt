Customer: Acme Technologies
Meeting: Engineering Onboarding Discussion
Date: 2026-08-04

Participants:
- Emily Carter, Director of Engineering
- Sameer Joshi, Engineering Manager
- Daniel Park, Senior Software Engineer
- Priya Shah, Customer Success Manager

Priya:
I'd like to understand how new engineers get productive when they join the
organization.

Emily:
That's definitely an area we'd like to improve. We hire engineers regularly,
and getting them productive takes longer than we'd like.

Sameer:
The biggest issue is that there's a lot they need to learn. They have to
understand our architecture, services, development process, and internal
tools.

Daniel:
When I joined, I spent a lot of time asking other engineers questions. Even
now, when someone new joins my team, I spend several hours helping them
understand the codebase.

Priya:
Is there formal documentation for this?

Sameer:
There is documentation, but it's spread across Confluence, GitHub, and
internal wikis. Some of it is useful, but it doesn't always explain how
everything fits together.

Emily:
We also have recorded training sessions, but people don't always know which
ones are relevant to their role.

Daniel:
A new engineer might ask me, "How does the payments service communicate with
the customer service?" I can explain it, but finding that answer in our
documentation can take much longer.

Priya:
How much time do experienced engineers spend helping new people?

Daniel:
Probably two to four hours a week during the first month for each new hire.

Sameer:
And that varies depending on how complicated the area is.

Emily:
I'd be interested in an AI assistant that could answer onboarding questions
using our internal documentation and codebase.

Daniel:
I'd use something like that, but it would need to show where the answer came
from. I wouldn't trust an answer without references.

Sameer:
It would also need to understand the person's role. A backend developer
doesn't need the same information as a QA engineer.

Priya:
Would you be comfortable piloting that?

Emily:
Yes. Start with a small group of new engineers and compare how quickly they
reach their first independent contribution.

Daniel:
I'd also measure how many questions they need to ask senior engineers.

Sameer:
If we can reduce the mentoring burden without making onboarding worse, that
would be a meaningful improvement.

Priya:
So the pilot could focus on engineering onboarding, with source-grounded
answers and role-specific guidance.

Emily:
Exactly.
