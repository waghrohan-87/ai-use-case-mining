Customer: Acme Technologies
Meeting: Developer Environment & Tooling Review
Date: 2026-08-06

Participants:
- Jonathan Reed, Director of Developer Experience
- Meera Kapoor, Engineering Manager
- Omar Hassan, Senior Software Engineer
- Priya Shah, Customer Success Manager

Priya:
I'd like to understand some of the recurring friction developers experience
before they can actually start working on a task.

Jonathan:
Developer environment setup is one area we've been trying to improve. New
developers and even existing developers sometimes spend a surprising amount
of time getting their local environment working.

Meera:
Every team has slightly different setup requirements. Someone might spend
half a day installing dependencies, configuring services, getting credentials,
and figuring out why something isn't working locally.

Omar:
And when something breaks, the error messages aren't always helpful. I
usually search Slack, GitHub issues, and old documentation to figure out what
someone else did.

Priya:
How often does this happen?

Omar:
For new developers, almost every time. For existing developers, probably a
few times a month when they move between services.

Jonathan:
We've created setup documentation, but it gets outdated as dependencies
change.

Meera:
The other problem is that developers often don't know whether the issue is
with their machine, the dependency, or the service configuration.

Priya:
Have you tried using AI to help troubleshoot these issues?

Omar:
I've used general AI tools to explain error messages. That's useful, but the
answers aren't always specific to our environment.

Jonathan:
I'd be interested in something that understands our internal setup
documentation and common environment issues.

Meera:
It could also guide developers through the setup step by step and identify
where their configuration differs from the expected environment.

Omar:
I'd want it to show the documentation or previous issue that supports its
recommendation.

Priya:
Would this be something you would pilot?

Jonathan:
Yes. I'd start with new developers because that's where we have the most
obvious time cost.

Meera:
We could measure time from account creation to a developer successfully
running their first service locally.

Omar:
And for existing developers, we could track how quickly recurring setup
issues get resolved.

Priya:
Would there be any concerns?

Jonathan:
We'd need to be careful with credentials and secrets. The AI should never
have access to sensitive credentials.

Priya:
Understood. We could keep the initial pilot focused on documentation and
troubleshooting guidance rather than automated environment changes.

Jonathan:
That would be a reasonable starting point.
